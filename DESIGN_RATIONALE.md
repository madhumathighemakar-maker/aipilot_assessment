# AdPilot — Design rationale

**Audience.** The page is designed for performance marketing managers who move between channel, campaign, creative, and budget tools to decide what deserves attention and what to do next.

**Central story.** AdPilot connects fragmented signals through one operating loop: Observe → Understand → Recommend → Execute → Learn. The line begins at the hero’s signal label, meets each stage, and ends at the closing CTA. The network becomes more connected as the story moves from signal to decision.

**Priorities.** A Monday review anchors the page in three situations: a high-spend campaign declining for three days, a budget-constrained campaign above target, and a TikTok creative worth testing on Meta. Selecting an insight updates its evidence and interpretation. The recommendation focuses on Summer Acquisition, with illustrative ROAS of 4.8× against a 3.5× target and a proposed 18% budget increase to evaluate.

**Visual system.** An off-white canvas, graphite type, restrained green accent, thin rules, and compact product panels keep the experience editorial and credible. Signal paths connect the sections without turning them into a feature grid.

**AI and trust.** AdPilot distinguishes observed signals from interpretations and proposed actions. Creative fatigue is framed as a hypothesis. The execution preview demonstrates Recommend, Approve, and Automate modes, including an example guardrail that routes an 18% change to human review. Approval changes local demo state only; no ad account or external action is connected. The request form also stays local.

**Omissions and next exploration.** I left out fabricated customer proof, live integrations, and a full advertising dashboard. With more time, I would validate the Monday scenario with media buyers and test how confidence, attribution uncertainty, and configurable permissions should appear before approval.
